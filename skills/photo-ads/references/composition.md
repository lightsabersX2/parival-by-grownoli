# Composition

An ad's composition is two choices made on purpose: how close the picture stands (framing) and what shape carries the eye (structure). The habit is one answer to both: the subject in the middle at medium distance, words in a band. Choose from the lists below before building, write both choices in `notes.md`, and in a set give no two ads the same pair.

## Framing: how close

| Framing | What it shows | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Detail | A part: an eye, a paw, a hand, bark, a petal | The texture or the look is the message; the brand is known enough to carry a fragment |
| Close-up | The face or the whole small subject, filling the frame | Feeling: a look that meets the viewer |
| Mid | The subject with some of its surroundings | The subject is doing something |
| Environmental | A small subject in a wide place | Scale, belonging, what would be lost |
| Wide, no subject | The place itself | The place is the subject: a forest, a coast, a meadow |

Crop the same photo three ways before settling: most photos hold a detail, a close-up and an environmental picture, and the first crop is rarely the strongest. A cut-off subject (half a face, a figure entering from the edge) is a choice, not a mistake, when the cut adds tension.

## Structure: what carries the eye

- **Rule of thirds.** Subject or horizon on a third line, the subject's eye on a crossing. The safe choice, so pick it last.
- **Golden section and golden spiral.** As thirds, tighter to one corner, with a curve of secondary things winding in to the subject.
- **Diagonal.** One strong line corner to corner: a branch, a slope, a shoreline. Energy and direction.
- **Double diagonal and golden triangles.** Two lines crossing off-centre; the subject sits in one triangle, the words in another.
- **Leading lines.** A road, a river, a fence or a gaze that enters at an edge and runs inward to the subject or the words.
- **S-curve.** A path or a stream winding back into depth. Calm, invites the eye to travel.
- **V and funnel.** Two masses opening onto the subject between them, or a frame inside the frame (a gap in trees, a doorway, a tunnel of branches).
- **L.** A tall mass on one side and a ground line: a trunk and a meadow. Leaves a large clear field for words.
- **C and circular.** The subject held by a curve or a ring of other things: foliage around a clearing.
- **Pyramid.** A broad base rising to a point. Stable, monumental: a mountain, a parent over a cub.
- **Radial.** Lines or rays spreading from one point: sun through trees, petals.
- **Cross.** A vertical through a horizon. Still, formal.
- **Focal mass.** Many small things and one dense cluster: a flock, a field of flowers.
- **Balance and imbalance.** Two equal weights for calm; one large and one small for tension and scale.
- **Pattern interrupt.** A repeated thing and one that breaks it: one red flower among white, one bird facing the other way. The break is where the eye lands, so put the message there.

## Words inside the composition

The words are a mass in the picture, not a layer on top. Place them where the structure leaves a field (the open side of an L, the empty triangle, the far end of a leading line) and let the subject's gaze or the picture's lines point at them. When the picture has no field, crop until it has one, or give the words their own ground beside the picture.

## Before building

Write one line in `notes.md`: "Framing: … Structure: … because …". If the line reads "mid, thirds", say what makes that right for this message, or choose again.

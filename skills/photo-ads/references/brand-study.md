# Brand study

A brand looks different on its start page, its campaign pages, its social feeds and its paid ads. The best of these, not the site's start page, sets the floor for an ad. Study each place and rank it on its own.

## Places

List every place the brand shows itself, one row each:

- the start page, and each campaign page, landing page or inner page that a campaign or a donation or sign-up flow uses;
- each organic social channel the brand runs (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube): the latest 30 to 50 posts, logged out, in a browser with no signed-in profile;
- the brand's live ads in Meta's ad library (facebook.com/ads/library), which is public.

Screenshots the person supplies count as a place.

## Browsers

Start headless. When a place hides posts or engagement behind a login wall, a bot check or a consent page, look again in a real browser: Brave, Chrome, Safari or the agent's built-in browser. Use a profile the person has approved for this work; a profile signed in to their employer stays untouched. The browser is for looking: read posts, scroll, open a post to see its numbers. Leave every account as it was: no likes, follows, comments, messages or sign-ins by the agent, and the person solves any bot check.

**A place that asks for sign-in is the person's call.** Stop and ask once, naming the place and what it would add, such as which of the brand's posts drew the most reactions. Offer three answers, with skipping first:

1. Skip it. The ad is made from the website alone and the place is noted as unseen.
2. The person signs in themselves, in the browser pane you opened, and tells you when they are in.
3. You look in the person's own Chrome through the Claude extension, when it is on.

Take the answer for every place in this run and record it in `brand.md`. The person types every password. A place that stays closed is noted as unseen, not guessed.

## Engagement

Engagement is the strongest signal of what works for this brand.

**Sample per channel:** the latest 30 posts from the past 12 months, fewer if the channel has fewer. Leave out pinned posts and posts under 7 days old, which are still collecting reactions. The typical post is the median of these, so one viral post does not move it. Study closely the 5 posts furthest above the median, the 3 furthest below, and the 3 latest, which show the current look. A channel with under 12 posts in the window is marked thin, and its scores carry less weight. The cap keeps the study the same size for every brand; recency keeps it about today's look.

For each post in the sample, note the reactions, comments, shares and views that are shown. Name the posts that clearly beat the typical one and what they share (subject, picture, tone, format, wit). In Meta's ad library, an ad that has run for weeks or in many versions is a sign it works. Record the numbers you saw and the date.

## Scores

Score each place 1 to 5 on each line, with one sentence of evidence that names what you saw:

- **picture**: how strong the photos or illustrations are (crop, light, subject, sharpness);
- **type**: hierarchy, size and how words sit on pictures;
- **colour**: whether colour is used with intent and in the brand's palette;
- **tone**: how the brand sounds, whether it has wit or warmth, and how much energy it sells with;
- **response** (social and ads only): which posts or ads drew clearly more reactions than the rest, and what they share.

Scores compare places of this one brand. They are not a verdict on the brand. The study is a baseline: it shows whether an ad is more of the same or goes further. A brand with weak socials, or one that wants something new, still gets an ad built for the message; the study only says where the bar is.

## brand-look.md

Write in the brand's folder:

1. A table of places by score, with the evidence.
2. **Take from:** for each line, the best place and what exactly to carry over.
3. **Gaps:** what is weak everywhere, such as empty space, flat hierarchy or small type, so the ad does better than the brand rather than copying the weakness.
4. **Seen on:** the date of the study and which places were unseen.

Keep it short: a maker reads it before every ad. Redo it when it is older than three months or the brand changes its look.
